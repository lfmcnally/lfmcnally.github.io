# Classicalia: the offer

> **Status:** proposal. This replaces the earlier structural redesign. It starts from what Classicalia should offer, to whom and in what formats, and lets the site follow from that. `SITE_REVIEW.md` holds the evidence about the current site. `docs/redesign/offer.html` is the visual summary.

---

## 1. The premise

There is no home for Classics at school. Every other subject has one or more of: a browsable public revision library (Bitesize), an exam-board revision and past-paper catalogue (Save My Exams, PMT), an adaptive practice platform with a teacher dashboard (Seneca, Sparx, Century), and a lesson library for teachers (Oak). For Classics the picture in October 2026 is:

- **Latin.** Save My Exams has OCR and Eduqas GCSE and A-level Latin: language revision notes, exam questions and past papers, behind its subscription. It does not do set texts. Oak has KS3 and KS4 Latin grammar units with video, worksheet and quiz, and has announced the removal of some of them. The Cambridge School Classics Project supports its own course and the Eduqas set texts for free. Nothing covers the OCR set texts, which change every two years and are half the GCSE.
- **Classical Civilisation.** Save My Exams lists the OCR specifications and past papers under History and has no notes. Massolit sells video lectures at £19 a month. Revision notes exist as £2 to £9 packs on Tes and in one freemium app. There is no free, complete, spec-aligned set of notes for any Class Civ component.
- **Greek.** Almost nothing anywhere beyond vocabulary flashcards.
- **Teachers.** Classics for All reports training more than 7,000 teachers, many of them non-specialists, and the Classical Association reports that state schools now account for nearly 45% of GCSE Latin entrants and rising. These teachers need ready-to-teach lessons, schemes of work and assessments more than any other group in UK education, and nothing like Oak exists for OCR Classics courses.
- **Practice and tracking.** No adaptive platform covers Classics. Vocabulary practice lives in user-made Quizlet and Brainscape decks.

Classicalia already has pieces of all four kinds of site, 400 users and 50 schools, and an author who is a practising teacher. The opportunity is to be the one place for Classics at school: the revision library, the practice platform and the teacher resource bank, free, for every OCR Classics qualification. That is a bigger claim than "free resources", and it decides everything that follows.

Sources: [Save My Exams OCR GCSE Latin](https://www.savemyexams.com/gcse/latin/ocr) · [Save My Exams OCR A-level Class Civ](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/exam-specifications/a-level/history/ocr/classical-civilisation/) · [Oak Latin units](https://www.thenational.academy/pupils/programmes/latin-secondary-year-9-l/units) · [Massolit subscriptions](https://massolit.io/subscribe) · [CSCP and Eduqas](https://www.cambridge.org/gb/files/1015/7900/1477/2020_Classics_Brochure.pdf) · [Tes Class Civ notes](https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/myth-and-religion-ocr-gcse-classical-civilisation-9-1-full-course-revision-notes-12953281) · [Adapt GCSE Class Civ](https://getadapt.co.uk/revision-content/classics/gcse-classical-civilisation-ocr) · [Classics for All impact report 2024](https://classicsforall.org.uk/sites/default/files/uploads/Annual%20reports/Classics%20for%20All%20-%20Impact%20Report%202024_DIGITAL.pdf) · [Classical Association on state-sector growth](https://classicalassociation.org/?p=31623)

## 2. Who it is for, and what each of them needs

| Audience | Situation | What they come for | What they get today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The revising student** | Year 11 or 13, OCR, exam in months, often no textbook beyond the set text | Notes for every spec point, the set text explained line by line, practice questions with mark schemes, a way to know what they still don't know | Strong vocab and quiz practice; excellent notes for a few topics; stubs for most; no exam practice with mark schemes; no set-text questions |
| **The student in a class** | Set work by a teacher who uses the site | Homework and Do Nows that just work, a clear list of what is due, feedback that comes back quickly | Works, but through a separate "app" the content side does not link to |
| **The teacher** | Increasingly non-specialist, in a state school, teaching Latin or Class Civ with little departmental support | Lessons ready to teach, a scheme of work for the current cycle, assessments with mark schemes, something to set for homework, a view of who is stuck | Some Odyssey slides and workbooks; a bank and AI marking hidden behind permissions; classroom tools; a class dashboard |
| **The beginner and the curious** | KS3 pupil, parent, student choosing options, adult learner | Why Classics, what the subjects are, a taste of Latin, a way in | A "why study Classics" page and a Christmas lesson, both unreachable from the live site |

The first and third groups are the ones no one else serves. The offer is built for them, and the second group is served as a consequence.

## 3. The offer

**Positioning.** Classicalia is the home of Classics at school: every OCR Classics qualification, revised, practised and taught in one place, free for every student.

**The promise to a student.** For your course you will find notes on every spec point, your set texts explained line by line, practice that remembers what you know, and exam questions with mark schemes and model answers. You can get from "what's on the paper" to "I can do this" without buying anything.

**The promise to a teacher.** For your course you will find lessons you can teach tomorrow, a scheme of work for the current cycle, assessments with mark schemes, and a class view that tells you who needs what. Set anything on the site as work in one click.

**The promise to the curious.** A free first lesson in Latin or Greek, an explanation of what Classics is and why it matters, and a daily piece of the ancient world.

What makes it defensible: completeness for Classics specifically (set texts, Greek, Class Civ, where the general sites stop), the classroom layer (which the revision catalogues do not have), and the author (a practising teacher, which the platforms do not have).

## 4. The formats

An offer is a set of formats with fixed shapes. Each format below has one job, one anatomy that every instance follows, and a status. Fixed anatomies are what make a site feel like one thing rather than many pages, and they are what make content producible at scale.

### For students

1. **Topic notes.** One page per specification point, in the exam board's own wording. Anatomy: what you need to know · sources and evidence · how to say it in the exam (a model paragraph) · five-question check · flashcards. About eight minutes. *Exists for about 50 topics in three templates; 284 stubs. Standardise on the Odyssey guide template.*
2. **Set-text companions.** One per prescribed text per exam cycle. Anatomy: the text in the parallel reader · vocabulary with a tester · literal translation · style notes by category · line commentary · context and themes · a quotation bank · practice questions (comprehension, 8 and 10 mark, translation check, scansion for verse). *Six texts exist across three readers; none has questions or a quotation bank. This is the format no competitor has.*
3. **Vocabulary.** Every defined list (GCSE and A-level Latin and Greek), every set text's words, the main KS3 course books. One tester, one memory. *Exists; merge the two testers.*
4. **Grammar.** A reference entry and a drill for every construction on the GCSE and A-level specifications. Anatomy: the rule · examples from the set texts · a drill · a check. *One chapter exists. Build the whole reference; it is finite and never changes.*
5. **Exam practice.** Question banks by topic and question type, with mark schemes and model answers at grade bands; timed papers assembled from them; AI feedback on extended writing (essays, translations, unseens) with limits for free users. *The bank, AI marking and exam builder exist; none is reachable by a student. Unseen translation with instant feedback would be unique.*
6. **Exam skills.** One guide per paper: structure, timing, command words, the mark scheme decoded, what examiners' reports keep saying. *Fragments exist.*
7. **Knowledge organisers and checklists.** One per course: every spec point on one printable page, tickable, each line linking to its notes. *None exist; cheap to generate from the notes once they have a standard anatomy.*
8. **Revision planner.** Pick your courses and exam dates and get a weekly plan that draws on the formats above. *A planner URL exists as a redirect; nothing behind it.*
9. **Progress.** One map across every course, smart practice from weakest and overdue items, and the sharing code. *Exists; the best part of the site.*

### For teachers

10. **Lesson packs.** One per topic: slides, a Do Now, a worksheet, a homework, teacher notes with the key misconceptions. The Oak model, for OCR courses. *Four Odyssey topics have slides and workbooks.*
11. **Schemes of work.** One per course per cycle, linking to the lesson packs and the set-text companions. *One exists, for the Odyssey.*
12. **Assessment packs.** End-of-topic tests, mocks and mark schemes, in the bank, assignable and AI-marked. *The bank exists; it has no content.*
13. **Cover lessons.** Self-contained lessons a non-specialist can run from the sheet. *One exists, for English.*
14. **Classroom tools.** Live Do Now, visualiser, exam paper builder, starter sheets, Look Cover Write Check. *Exist and are good.*
15. **Class tracking.** Classes, set work, results, insights. *Exists.*
16. **CPD.** Short pieces on teaching Classics (the blog and the INSET deck are the seed), aimed at non-specialists. *Exists but unreachable.*

### For everyone

17. **A first lesson** in Latin and in Greek, free, no account. *The Christmas lesson is the model.*
18. **Why Classics.** The options-evening page, with the case for each subject. *Exists, unreachable.*
19. **Today in the ancient world.** The daily events feed on the homepage. *The data file exists, unused.*

### What to stop

Formats that cost attention and do not serve the promise: the 3D games (20 MB, two players at a time, one chapter), friends leaderboards (a social feature on a site with no social layer), seasonal skins, the soundboard and symbols pages, the whiteboard and lesson-builder experiments, the English resources. Park them under a "lab" page or retire them. The energy goes into the formats above.

## 5. Coverage: the promise has to be true

The current site advertises eight Class Civ components as available and has written 48 of 332 lessons. One author cannot write everything, so the offer has to be honest about what is complete and deliberate about the order.

**Rule:** a course is listed when a student can revise it end to end from the site: notes for every spec point, companions for the current set texts, practice, exam practice and a knowledge organiser. Until then it is "in progress" with a visible percentage, and its finished topics are still usable.

**Order.** Finish before starting: GCSE Latin (language spec complete, both cycle-5 set texts, grammar reference) and GCSE Class Civ Myth and Religion and The Homeric World, because they have the most students and the least competition. Then A-level Class Civ The Odyssey and Greek Art, A-level Latin set texts, then the remaining GCSE Civ components, then GCSE Greek, then the remaining A-level Civ options. Ancient History (OCR J198 and H407) is the natural fourth subject once the first three are complete.

**A public coverage board** on the site: every course, every format, percentage complete, what is next. It is a promise students can hold the site to and a recruiting tool for contributors.

## 6. Free and school

Everything a student uses alone stays free: notes, companions, vocabulary, grammar, exam practice with mark schemes, progress, the planner, and a monthly allowance of AI feedback. Everything a teacher needs to teach stays free with a verified teacher account: lesson packs, schemes of work, cover lessons, classroom tools, classes and tracking.

A **school plan** pays for what has a real per-use cost or a curation cost: AI marking at class scale, the assessment bank and mocks, exports and reports, and support. The line is drawn so that a free student never meets a wall, and a school pays for the things a school would otherwise pay a human for.

Supporters remain, as one ask per page.

## 7. Making it

The offer is only credible with a production model that does not depend on one person's evenings.

- **Fixed anatomies.** Every format has a template and a checklist. A contributor writes into the shape; the site renders it.
- **Contributors.** Classics for All's network, ACE, PGCE cohorts and subject associations are full of teachers who would write one topic each under editorial review, with credit. Oak and Bitesize are written this way. The author becomes the editor.
- **AI-assisted drafting, teacher-edited.** The site already uses Claude to mark. The same can produce a first draft of a topic note from the specification, a source list and the existing workbook, for a teacher to edit. Marked as reviewed before it is published.
- **One manifest, one build.** Every course, topic, text and format is described in one data file; the site, the coverage board, the planner, the set-work picker and the sitemap are generated from it. This is what keeps forty templates from reappearing.
- **Evidence.** Keep the mastery data. Over a year it shows which words, grammar points and questions students in fifty schools find hardest, which is a publishable finding and a guide to what to write next.

## 8. How the site follows

With the offer fixed, the site is short to describe. Six areas, one header.

- **Home.** What Classicalia is, in a sentence, for each of the three audiences. The course picker. Today in the ancient world.
- **Courses.** One page per qualification, listing its formats with coverage: notes, set texts, vocabulary, grammar, exam practice, exam skills, knowledge organiser, planner, lesson packs. The course page is a catalogue of the offer for that course, which is a different thing from the hub of lesson links it is today.
- **Revise.** The library of notes and companions across courses, searchable, in one reader.
- **Practise.** Vocabulary, grammar, content questions and exam practice, with one memory and smart practice across courses.
- **Teach.** Classes, set work, results, lesson packs, schemes, assessments, cover lessons, tools, CPD.
- **Discover.** Why Classics, a first lesson, today in the ancient world, the blog.

Signed in, Home becomes your day across courses and Progress sits beside it. The visual system can stay close to the current identity or move to the lighter product surface sketched in `docs/redesign/concept.html`; that choice is secondary to the offer and can be made later.

## 9. Decisions

1. **The claim.** "The home of Classics at school", with the promises in section 3, or a narrower one.
2. **The format list** in section 4, and in particular adding exam practice, knowledge organisers, grammar reference and lesson packs as first-class formats.
3. **The stop list.**
4. **The coverage rule and order** in section 5, including whether Ancient History is on the roadmap.
5. **The free and school line** in section 6, in particular keeping lesson packs free for verified teachers.
6. **The production model**: contributors under editorial review and AI-assisted drafting.

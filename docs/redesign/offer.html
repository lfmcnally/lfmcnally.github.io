Classicalia: the home of Classics at school

Every OCR Classics qualification, revised, practised and taught in one place, free for every student. This page sets out what the site should offer, to whom, in what formats, and how it is made. The site's shape follows from that, not the other way round.

Nobody owns this

Every other subject has a public revision library, an exam-board catalogue, a practice platform and a lesson library. Classics has fragments of each, mostly paid, and no set-text, Greek or Class Civ notes anywhere.

Meanwhile Classics for All reports more than 7,000 teachers trained, many of them non-specialists, and state schools now account for nearly 45% of GCSE Latin entrants and rising. The people who most need ready-made lessons and complete notes are the fastest-growing part of the subject.

The promise

To a student

For your course: notes on every spec point, your set texts explained line by line, practice that remembers what you know, and exam questions with mark schemes and model answers. From "what's on the paper" to "I can do this", without buying anything.

To a teacher

For your course: lessons you can teach tomorrow, a scheme of work for the current cycle, assessments with mark schemes, and a class view that tells you who needs what. Set anything on the site as work in one click.

To the curious

A free first lesson in Latin or Greek, an honest account of what Classics is and why it matters, and a piece of the ancient world every day.

Who it is for

The revising student
Year 11 or 13, OCR, exam in months. Needs every spec point, the set text line by line, practice questions with mark schemes, and a way to know what they still don't know.
Today: strong vocab and quiz practice; excellent notes for a few topics; stubs for most; no exam practice; no set-text questions.
The student in a class
Set work by a teacher who uses the site. Needs homework and Do Nows that just work, one list of what is due, quick feedback.
Today: works, through a separate app the content side does not link to.
The teacher
Increasingly non-specialist, in a state school, little departmental support. Needs lessons ready to teach, a scheme of work, assessments, homework to set, a view of who is stuck.
Today: some Odyssey slides; a bank and AI marking hidden behind permissions; good tools; a class dashboard.
The beginner and the curious
KS3 pupil, parent, student choosing options, adult learner. Needs a reason and a way in.
Today: a "why Classics" page and a Christmas lesson, both unreachable.

Coverage: the promise has to be true

A course is listed as complete when a student can revise it end to end from the site. Until then it shows its percentage and its finished topics are usable. Finish before starting: the courses with the most students and the least competition come first. A public board like this one holds the site to its word and recruits contributors.

Free, and what a school pays for

Free, for everyone
  • All notes, companions, vocabulary, grammar, exam skills, knowledge organisers, the planner
  • Exam practice with mark schemes and model answers, and a monthly allowance of AI feedback
  • Progress and smart practice
  • With a verified teacher account: lesson packs, schemes of work, cover lessons, classroom tools, classes and tracking
The line: a free student never meets a wall, and a teacher can teach the course from the site.
School plan
  • AI marking at class scale: weekly tests, essays, unseens
  • The assessment bank and mocks
  • Exports, reports and department views
  • Support and onboarding
The line: a school pays for what has a real per-use cost or would otherwise cost a person's time.

Making it

The offer is only credible with a production model that does not depend on one person's evenings.

Fixed anatomiesEvery format has a template and a checklist. A contributor writes into the shape; the site renders it.
ContributorsClassics for All's network, ACE, PGCE cohorts: one topic each, under editorial review, with credit. Oak and Bitesize are written this way.
AI-drafted, teacher-editedThe marking engine's model drafts a topic note from the spec, a source list and the workbook. A teacher edits; nothing publishes unreviewed.
One manifestEvery course, topic, text and format in one data file. The site, the coverage board, the planner and the set-work picker are generated from it.
EvidenceA year of mastery data from fifty schools shows which words, constructions and questions are hardest: a guide to what to write next, and a publishable finding.

The site, in six areas

HomeWhat it is, for each audience. The course picker. Today in the ancient world.
CoursesOne offer sheet per qualification, with coverage.
ReviseNotes and companions across courses, one reader, searchable.
PractiseVocabulary, grammar, questions, exam practice. One memory.

DiscoverWhy Classics, a first lesson, the blog.

Signed in, Home becomes your day across courses and Progress sits beside it. Whether the look stays close to the current identity or moves to a lighter product surface is a later decision; the offer is the thing to agree first.

Decisions

  1. The claim. "The home of Classics at school", with the three promises, or something narrower.
  2. The format list, in particular exam practice, knowledge organisers, the grammar reference and lesson packs as first-class formats.
  3. The stop list.
  4. The coverage rule and the order, including whether Ancient History is on the roadmap.
  5. The free and school line, in particular lesson packs free for verified teachers.
  6. The production model: contributors under editorial review, and AI-assisted drafting.

Latin
1conticuere omnes intentique ora tenebant.
2inde toro pater Aeneas sic orsus ab alto:
3infandum, regina, iubes renovare dolorem,
4Troianas ut opes et lamentabile regnum
5eruerint Danai; quaeque ipse miserrima vidi,
6et quorum pars magna fui. quis talia fando
7Myrmidonum Dolopumve aut duri miles Ulixi
8temperet a lacrimis? et iam nox umida caelo
9praecipitat, suadentque cadentia sidera somnos.
10sed si tantus amor casus cognoscere nostros
11et breviter Troiae supremum audire laborem,
12quamquam animus meminisse horret luctuque refugit,
13incipiam. fracti bello fatisque repulsi
English
1They all fell silent, and fixed their eyes attentively on him.
2Then father Aeneas, having risen from his tall couch, began:
3O queen, you order me to renew an unspeakable grief,
4how the Greeks destroyed the Trojan wealth and the lamentable kingdom;
5and those most wretched things which I myself saw,
6and of which I was a great part. Who, in telling such things,
7of the Myrmidons or the Dolopians, or a soldier of harsh Ulysses,
8could hold back from tears? And already the damp night falls from the sky
9and the setting stars urge sleep.
10But if your desire to know our downfall is so great,
11and briefly to hear the final struggle of Troy,
12although my mind shudders to remember and has recoiled in grief,
13I shall begin. Broken by war and driven back by the fates,

Style notes this section6
1
conticuereAdvancement
The verb comes first: the silence falls before we know who is silent.
1
omnesEmphatic
Everyone, the whole court, held by the story to come.
2
paterEpithet
Indicates responsibility as well as respect: Aeneas is a father before he is a hero.
2
toro … altoHyperbaton
The couch and its height frame the line, as they frame the speaker.
9
suadentque cadentiaAssonance
The repeated -ent- sounds slow the line as night comes on.
9
sidera somnosAlliteration
A soft close to the line: the stars and sleep together.

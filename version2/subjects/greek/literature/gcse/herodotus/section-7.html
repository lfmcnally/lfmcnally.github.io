Oracles at Dodona and Delphi background

When a Greek community met a disaster it could not explain, such as plague, failed harvests or barren flocks, it commonly sent to an oracle to ask which god was angry and what would put things right. Delphi, Apollo's sanctuary on the slopes of Mount Parnassus, was the most prestigious: there the god answered through his priestess, the Pythia. Dodona, in Epirus in north-west Greece, was an oracle of Zeus, said to be the oldest in Greece, where the god's will was linked with a sacred oak. Excavators there have found thousands of small lead tablets on which ordinary people scratched their questions: about marriages, journeys, illnesses and lost property, as well as the affairs of cities. Consulting two oracles and getting the same answer from both left the Apollonians in no doubt.

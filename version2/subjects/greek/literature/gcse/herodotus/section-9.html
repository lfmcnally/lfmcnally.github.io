65
οὐκ ἀκηκοὼς τὸ θεοπρόπιονDramatic irony
not having heard the oracle
The reader knows the gods have promised him whatever he asks, but Euenius does not, so every word of his request is read with that in mind.
66–67
κάλλιστοι … καλλίστηνSuperlative
finest … finest
He asks for the very best land and the very best house, which shows he is aiming high.
66–68
κάλλιστοι ὄντες τῶν ἐν τῇ Ἀπολλωνίᾳ … καλλίστην οὖσαν τῶν ἐν πόλειParallelism
the finest in Apollonia … the finest in the town
The two phrases are built the same way, so the land and the house make a neat matching pair.
67
πρὸς τούτοιςEmphasis
on top of these
He adds a house to the land, so the reader sees his demand growing as he speaks.
67
ἣν ᾔδειCharacterisation
which he knew
Euenius knows exactly which house is the finest, which makes his request precise and personal.
68
ἀμήνιτοςCharacterisation
without resentment
The word comes from μῆνις, the wrath of gods and heroes, which suggests how deep his anger has been.
68–69
ἀμήνιτος ἂν εἴη … ἂν ἀποχρῴηParallelism
he would be without resentment … it would be enough
Two matching clauses with ἄν close his request, so he offers to end the matter for good.
69–70
δίκη … δίκηνRepetition
compensation … compensation
The citizens pick up Euenius' own word at once, which shows they are holding him to it.
69
ὁ μὲν … οἱ δὲContrast (μέν ... δέ)
he … but they
Euenius speaks and the citizens answer at once, so the reader feels the trap snap shut.
70
ὑπολαβόντεςFast pace
taking him up
They reply the moment he finishes, which gives him no time to change his mind.
70
"ΕὐήνιεDirect speech
Euenius
The reveal is given in the citizens' own words, so the reader hears exactly what Euenius hears.
70
ταύτην τὴν δίκηνEmphatic position
this compensation
The object comes first in their reply, so the stress falls on the payment he has just agreed to.
71
ἐκτίνουσιIrony
are paying in full
They claim to be paying in full, yet they are paying only what a tricked man asked for.
71–72
κατὰ θεοπρόπια τὰ γενόμεναEmphatic position
in accordance with the oracles
The oracles are mentioned last, so the twist comes at the very end of the sentence, just as it hits Euenius.

Euenius · lines 41–49

Wolves kill sixty of the sacred sheep while Euenius sleeps; he tries to hide the loss, and the Apollonians blind him · Tales from Herodotus · GCSE Greek · OCR J292/02

41
κατακοιμηθέντοςCharacterisation
having fallen asleep
The guard falls asleep on duty, so the reader sees Euenius fail at the very task he was chosen for.
41
κατακοιμηθέντος, λύκοιJuxtaposition
fallen asleep, wolves
The sleeping guard is placed right next to the wolves, so danger arrives the moment he drops off.
42
εἰσελθόντες διέφθειρανFast pace
came in and killed
A participle and a verb tell the whole attack in two words, so it happens as suddenly as it would in reality.
42
ἑξήκονταEmphatic position
sixty
The number comes last in the sentence, which brings home the size of the loss.
42–44
ὁ δέ … οἱ δὲContrast
he … but they
The story switches from Euenius alone to the whole city, which sets one man against his community.
43
εἶχε σιγῇ καὶ ἔφραζεν οὐδενίRepetition
he kept quiet and told no one
The same idea is said twice, positively and negatively, which shows that the cover-up is deliberate.
43
οὐδενίEmphatic negative
no one
"No one" is a strong word, which shows he told absolutely nobody.
44
ἀντικαταστήσειν ἄλλα πριάμενοςCharacterisation
to replace them by buying others
Euenius thinks sacred animals can simply be bought and swapped, which shows his lack of respect for the gods.
45
οὐ γὰρ ἔλαθεν αὐτοὺςLitotes
it did not escape them
An understatement that means they knew perfectly well, so his cover-up looks hopeless from the start.
46
ὑπαγαγόντες … κατέκρινανFast pace
they took him to court and condemned him
Trial and verdict are packed into one participle and one verb, which makes the city's justice seem hasty.
46–47
τῆς ὄψεως στερηθῆναιEmphatic position
to lose his sight
The punishment comes at the very end of the sentence, so the reader is struck by its cruelty.
48–49
ἐξετύφλωσαν … αὐτοῖςIrony
they blinded … for them
The Apollonians punish Euenius but bring suffering on themselves, so the reader suspects they were wrong.
48
αὐτίκα μετὰ ταῦταEmphasis
immediately afterwards
The blight follows the blinding at once, so the reader links the two even before the oracle explains it.
49
οὔτε πρόβατα … ἔτικτεν οὔτε γῆ ἔφερενParallelism
neither did the sheep bear … nor did the land yield
Two matching clauses cover both flocks and fields, which shows that the whole community is suffering.

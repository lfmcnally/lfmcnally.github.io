Blindness and prophecy background

Greek myth often pairs the loss of sight with a different kind of sight. Teiresias, the most famous seer of all, is blind: in one version Hera struck him blind and Zeus, unable to undo another god's act, gave him prophecy in return. The prophet Phineus was blinded too, and Homer's bard Demodocus in the Odyssey lost his eyes but was given the gift of song. Euenius fits this pattern: the gods cannot give back his eyes, so they give him an inner sight instead. Prophecy often ran in families in Greece, and Herodotus tells the story because Euenius' son Deiphonus was serving as a seer with the Greek fleet in 479 BC.

The oracle at Branchidae background

Didyma lay on the coast of Asia Minor about 15 kilometres south of Miletus, linked to the city by a sacred road. Its oracle of Apollo was run by the Branchidae, a priestly family who traced their descent from Branchus, a young man loved by the god, and the sanctuary was often called by their name.

It was one of the most respected oracles in the Greek world: Herodotus tells us that Croesus of Lydia sent it rich offerings. When the Ionian cities rebelled against Persia, the Persians took their revenge by plundering and burning the sanctuary (494 BC). The enormous temple whose ruins visitors see today was begun around 300 BC, long after the events of this story.

What became of Pactyes background

The Cymaeans found a way to avoid both handing Pactyes over and keeping him: they sent him away to Mytilene on Lesbos. When they heard that the Mytilenaeans were about to sell him to Mazares, they moved him again, to the island of Chios. There he took refuge as a suppliant in the temple of Athena, Guardian of the City.

It did not save him. The Chians dragged him out of the temple and gave him up to the Persians; their reward was Atarneus, a stretch of land on the mainland opposite Lesbos. Herodotus adds that for a long time afterwards no Chian would use barley grown at Atarneus in any offering to the gods: land bought at the price of a suppliant was polluted.

Set beside this story, the ending is pointed. The god at Branchidae had told the Cymaeans what happens to those who give up a suppliant, and the Chians did exactly that.

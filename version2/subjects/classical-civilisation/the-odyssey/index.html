The Odyssey

The compulsory epic, taught across a year in six topics — one per half-term, following the poem in order. Prescribed books: 1, 5–13, 16–19 and 21–23. Every book has an annotated reading workbook to download; the unprescribed books are marked as bridges — useful background for 30-mark essays, never examined in context questions. Each topic carries a lead theme taught explicitly, while other threads are seeded and revisited as the poem unfolds. The full scheme of work sets out the 37-lesson spine.

Key
★Lead theme — taught explicitly in that topic
⇩Workbook — annotated reading text for one book, free to download
↻Threads seeded or revisited across topics
▣Topic Tester — knowledge check at the end of a topic
✎Essay checkpoint — 30-mark essay under exam conditions
◦Bridge — unprescribed books in summary; can support and enrich 30-mark essays, but detailed evidence must come from the prescribed books

    Lesson spine
    1
    Xenia — the guest–host code Books 1, 3–4
    The code governing host and guest, and why it drives the poem. Read the suitors feasting in the hall, and Nestor's welcome at Pylos.
    2
    Nostos — the homecoming Book 1
    The poem's goal and organising idea: what Odysseus seeks, what blocks him, and how a good return differs from a failed one.
    3
    Kleos & timê — fame and honour Proem, Book 1
    The values a Homeric hero lives by, and how the wish for fame shapes a character's choices.
    4
    The gods, fate and responsibility Book 1
    Athene, Zeus and Poseidon, and the Orestes paradigm in Zeus' opening speech — where the justice thread is planted.
    5
    Mêtis — cunning intelligence Proem, Book 1
    Odysseus' defining quality set against biê (force) — ‘the man of many turns’, and Athene disguised as Mentes.
    6
    Disguise and recognition Books 1, 4
    The mechanism that drives the poem: the token, the dramatic irony, and Telemachus known by his likeness.
    7
    Storytelling, bards and oral style Book 1
    Formulae, epithets and the internal bard — Phemius singing the returns, and Penelope's response.
    8
    Women in society — Helen Book 4
    How the poem first represents women and their power, and the female thread that Topics 2, 3 and 5 develop.

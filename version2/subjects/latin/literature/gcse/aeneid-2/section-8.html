220īllĕ sĭmūl mănĭbūs tēndīt dīvēllĕrĕ nōdōs,
221pērfūsūs sănĭē vīttās ātrōquĕ vĕnēnō,
222clāmōrēs sĭmŭl hōrrēndōs ād sīdĕră tōllit:
223quālēs mūgītūs, fūgīt cūm saūcĭŭs āram
224taūrŭs, ĕt īncērtam͜ ēxcūssīt cērvīcĕ sĕcūrim.
220While he strains with his hands to unleash the knots
221his sacred ribbons drenched with gore and black venom,
222at the same time, he raises horrendous shouts to the stars:
223The lowing [is] like when a wounded bull flees the altar
224and shook off the unsteady axe from its neck.
225āt gĕmĭnī lāpsū dēlūbra͜ ād sūmmă drăcōnes
226ēffŭgĭūnt saēvaēquĕ pĕtūnt Trītōnĭdĭs ārcem,
227sūb pĕdĭbūsquĕ dĕaē clīpeīquĕ sŭb ōrbĕ tĕgūntur.
225but the twin snakes fled sliding away to the highest
226sanctuaries and making for the citadel of cruel Pallas,
227they conceal themselves under the feet of the goddess and below the circle of her shield.
228tūm vērō trĕmĕfāctă nŏvūs pēr pēctŏră cūnctis
229īnsĭnŭāt păvŏr, ēt scĕlŭs ēxpēndīssĕ mĕrēntem
230Lāŏcŏōntă fĕrūnt, sācrūm quī cūspĭdĕ rōbur
231laēsĕrĭt, ēt tērgō scĕlĕrātam͜ īntōrsĕrĭt hāstam.
228Indeed then a new fear creeps through the trembling hearts of all
229and they say that Laocoon paid the penalty for his crime, deservedly
230Since he wounded the sacred timbers with his spear
231and at its back hurled his wicked spear.
232dūcēndum͜ ād sēdēs sĭmŭlācrum͜ ōrāndăquĕ dīvaē
233nūmĭnă cōnclāmānt.
232And they all shout together that the image should be taken to the temple
233and divine favour begged of the goddess

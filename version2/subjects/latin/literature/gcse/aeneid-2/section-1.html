1
They all fell silent, and fixed their eyes attentively on him.
2
Then father Aeneas, having risen from his tall couch [said]
3
O queen, you order [me] to repeat unspeakable pain,
4
how the Danaans destroyed the Trojan wealth and lamentable kingdom;
5
and those most wretched things I have seen myself,
6
and of which I have been a large part. Who, of the Myrmidons
7
or of the Dolopes, or even a soldier of harsh Ulysses speaking such things
8
could abstain from tears? and already the damp night is falling
9
from the sky and the setting stars are urging us to sleep.
10
But if your desire is so great to learn of our downfall
11
and to hear briefly the last agony of Troy,
12
Although my mind shudders to have remembered and fled with grief,
13
I shall begin. Broken by war and rejected by fate,
14
the leaders of the Danaans, when so many years had already slipped by,
15
built a horse like a mountain with the divine skill of Athena
16
and they wove the ribs from cut down fir:
17
They pretend it is a votive for their return; that is the rumour that is spread.
18
In this they secretly enclose a body of men chosen by lot
19
within its dark sides, and they fill the cavernous insides
20
and its huge belly with armed soldiers.
21
There is, within sight of Tenedos, the island famous
22
in stories, rich in wealth, while the kingdoms of Priam were standing,
23
now it is only a bay and a not-so-good standing for boats:
24
Here they settled themsleves, having been carried onto the deserted shore.
25
We reasoned they had left and had sought Mycenae with the wind.
26
Therefore all of Troy released itself from the long sorrow;
27
The gates are opened; It is pleasing to go and to see the Doric camp
28
and the deserted places and abandoned shore.
29
Here band of Dolopians, here fierce Achilles was encamped;
30
Here was the place for the fleets, here they used to fight in battle array.
31
Some are astounded by the fatal gift for virgin Minerva,
32
and wonder at the size of the horse; and Thymoetes first
33
encourages it to be led within the walls and to be placed in the citadel,
34
either through deceit or the fates of Troy were already leaning thus.
35
But Capys, and those of better sense of mind,
36
suspected traps of the Greeks and order that they throw the gifts
37
headlong into the sea or they burn them, having kindled them with flames,
38
or to bore into the hollows of the belly and try for hiding places.
39
The uncertain people are split into contrary desires.

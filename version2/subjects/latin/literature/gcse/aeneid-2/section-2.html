The wooden horse background

After ten years of war the Greeks still could not take Troy. With the help of Athena (Minerva to the Romans) they built a huge wooden horse, hid their best warriors inside it, and left it on the shore as if it were an offering to the goddess for a safe journey home. The rest of the fleet sailed only as far as the island of Tenedos and waited out of sight.

The story was famous long before Virgil: in Homer's Odyssey the bard Demodocus sings of it, and Odysseus remembers the long wait inside the horse. Virgil tells it from the Trojans' side, as a tale of deceit and disaster.

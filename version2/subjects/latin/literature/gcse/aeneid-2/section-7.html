Laocoön
A Trojan priest of Neptune who had warned the Trojans not to trust the horse and had hurled his spear into its side (lines 40–53). He was sacrificing a bull to Neptune on the shore when the snakes came.

The Laocoön statue background

The death of Laocoön and his sons is also shown in one of the most famous sculptures of the ancient world, a marble group found in Rome in 1506 and now in the Vatican Museums. The writer Pliny the Elder saw it in the palace of the emperor Titus and names three sculptors from Rhodes: Hagesander, Polydorus and Athenodorus.

Scholars still argue whether the statue inspired Virgil, Virgil inspired the statue, or both go back to an earlier Greek source. If you can, look at a picture of it and compare how the sculptor and the poet each show the father's struggle and the sons' suffering.

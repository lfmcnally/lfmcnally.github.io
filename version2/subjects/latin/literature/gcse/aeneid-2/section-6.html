Between the two passages: Sinon background

The set text skips lines 57 to 194. In them, shepherds drag in a young Greek with his hands tied: Sinon. He tells the Trojans a carefully made-up story. He says the Greeks were about to sacrifice him, that he escaped, and that he now hates them. The horse, he claims, is an offering to Minerva to make up for the theft of her sacred statue, the Palladium, and it was built huge on purpose so that the Trojans could not take it into their city: if they did, Troy would one day defeat Greece. Priam believes him and has his bonds cut. The second passage begins as the Trojans accept the lie.

25We reasoned they had left and had sought Mycenae with the wind.

27pāndūntūr pōrtaē; iŭvăt īre͜ ēt Dōrĭcă cāstră

27The gates are opened; It is pleasing to go and to see the Doric camp
28and the deserted places and abandoned shore.

30Here was the place for the fleets, here they used to fight in battle array.

31Some are astounded by the fatal gift for virgin Minerva,
32and wonder at the size of the horse; and Thymoetes first
33encourages it to be led within the walls and to be placed in the citadel,
34either through deceit or the fates of Troy were already leaning thus.
35āt Căpўs, ēt quōrūm mĕlĭōr sēntēntĭă mēntĭ,
36aūt pĕlăgō Dănaŭm īnsĭdĭās sūspēctăquĕ dōnă
37praācĭpĭtāre͜ iūbēnt, sūbiēctīsque͜ ūrĕrĕ flāmmīs,
38aūt tĕrĕbrārĕ căvās ŭtĕri͜ ēt tēmptārĕ lătēbrās.
35But Capys, and those of better sense of mind,
36suspected traps of the Greeks and order that they throw the gifts
37headlong into the sea or they burn them, having kindled them with flames,
38or to bore into the hollows of the belly and try for hiding places.
39scīndĭtŭr īncērtūm stŭdĭa͜ īn cōntrārĭă vōlgŭs.
39The uncertain people are split into contrary desires.

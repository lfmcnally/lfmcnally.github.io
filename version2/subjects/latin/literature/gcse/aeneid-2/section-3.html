25We thought they had left and had sought Mycenae with the wind.
26Therefore all of Troy released itself from the long sorrow;
27pāndūntūr pōrtaē; iŭvăt īre͜ ēt Dōrĭcă cāstră
28dēsērtōsquĕ vĭdērĕ lŏcōs lītūsquĕ rĕlīctŭm.
27The gates are opened; it is a pleasure to go out and see the Doric camp
28and the deserted places and abandoned shore.
29hīc Dŏlŏpūm mănŭs, hīc saēvūs tēndēbăt Ăchīllēs;
29Here the band of Dolopes camped, here fierce Achilles pitched his tent;
30clāssĭbŭs hīc lŏcŭs; hīc ăciĕ cērtārĕ sŏlēbānt.
30Here was the place for the fleets, here they used to fight in battle array.
31pārs stŭpĕt īnnūptaē dōnum͜ ēxĭtĭālĕ Mĭnērvaē,
32ēt mōlēm mīrāntŭr ĕquī; prīmūsquĕ Thy̆moētĕs
33dūci͜ īntrā mūrōs hōrtātŭr ĕt ārcĕ lŏcārĭ,
34sīvĕ dŏlō, seū iām Troīaē sīc fātă fĕrēbānt.
31Some are astounded by the fatal gift for virgin Minerva,
32and wonder at the size of the horse; and Thymoetes first
33encourages it to be led within the walls and to be placed in the citadel,
34whether by trickery, or because the fates of Troy were already tending this way.
35āt Căpy̆s, ēt quōrūm mĕlĭōr sēntēntĭă mēntĭ,
36aūt pĕlăgō Dănaŭm īnsĭdĭās sūspēctăquĕ dōnă
37praēcĭpĭtāre͜ iūbēnt, sūbiēctīsque͜ ūrĕrĕ flāmmīs,
38aūt tĕrĕbrārĕ căvās ŭtĕri͜ ēt tēmptārĕ lătēbrās.
35But Capys, and those whose minds had better judgement,
36order us either to throw the trap of the Greeks and the suspicious gifts
37headlong into the sea, or to burn them with flames put underneath,
38or to bore into and probe the hollow hiding places of the belly.
39scīndĭtŭr īncērtūm stŭdĭa͜ īn cōntrārĭă vōlgŭs.
39The uncertain crowd is split into opposing views.

Questions to think about · 3
  1. Why are the Trojans so happy to explore the Greek camp? How does Virgil make their relief clear?
  2. Compare the advice of Thymoetes and Capys. Which does Aeneas, looking back, want us to agree with?
  3. How does Virgil show that the Trojan people are divided at the end of this section?
The deserted camp background

For ten years the Greeks had camped on the beach below Troy, with their ships drawn up on the shore. When the Trojans wake to find the camp empty and the fleet gone, they believe the war is over. In fact the Greek ships are hiding behind Tenedos, a few miles away.

The Trojans' first reaction is joy and curiosity: they wander round the camp pointing out where Achilles and his men had their tents. Then they come to the horse, and the city has to decide what to do with it. This debate is the first of several moments when Troy could still have been saved.

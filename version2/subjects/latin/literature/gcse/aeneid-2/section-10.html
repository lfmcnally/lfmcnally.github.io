Notes on the text 4
247
dei iussuNote
Apollo granted Cassandra the ability to predict the future to convince her to sleep with him, when she changed her mind, he cursed her to never be believed.
247
TeucrisThe Trojans
by the Trojans
The Teucri are the Trojans, named after Teucer, one of the first kings of Troy.
249
festa velamus frondeFestive garlands
we wreathe with festive foliage
Hanging leafy garlands on temples was what people did at festivals of thanksgiving. The Trojans believe the war is over and are giving thanks to the gods.
252
Myrmidonumque dolosThe Myrmidons
and the tricks of the Myrmidons
The Myrmidons were Achilles' soldiers, led at Troy after his death by his son Neoptolemus. Here they stand for the Greeks in general, hidden in the horse.
Questions to think about · 4
  1. How does Virgil make the Trojans' celebrations seem tragic in lines 248–249?
  2. How does the description of nightfall in lines 250–253 build tension?
  3. Looking back over the whole set text, who or what is most to blame for the fall of Troy: the Greeks, the gods, or the Trojans themselves?
  4. How does Virgil make us sympathise with the Trojans even as they make terrible mistakes?
What happens next background

The set text ends with Troy asleep. Later that night the Greek fleet sails back from the island of Tenedos, where it had been hiding, and Sinon lets the Greek warriors out of the horse. They kill the guards and open the gates to their army.

The ghost of Hector appears to Aeneas in a dream and tells him to flee and take Troy's gods with him. Aeneas fights at first, but after seeing King Priam killed he escapes, carrying his old father Anchises on his shoulders and leading his young son Ascanius by the hand. His wife Creusa is lost in the confusion, and her ghost tells him of the new home that awaits him in the west.

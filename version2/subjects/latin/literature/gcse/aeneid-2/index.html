Aeneid II

Virgil, Aeneid II.1–56 and 195–253 — the set text for Verse Literature B (cycle 5, examination 2027 & 2028). 115 lines in ten sections.

Lines 57–194 are not prescribed: between the two passages Sinon talks his way inside the walls. The second passage picks up at the moment his lie is believed.

Lines 1–56 The wooden horse and Laocoön’s warning
  • Lesson
    Section 1 — Lines 1–13 — Aeneas begins his story
    Silence falls over Dido’s hall. Aeneas protests that the queen is asking him to reopen an unspeakable grief — and begins anyway.
  • Lesson
    Section 2 — Lines 14–24 — The horse is built
    Beaten back by war and by fate, the Greek leaders build a horse the size of a mountain, hide picked men in its belly, and take the fleet out of sight behind Tenedos.
  • Lesson
    Section 3 — Lines 25–39 — The Trojans pour out; the horse debated
    Believing the Greeks gone, the Trojans throw open the gates and wander the abandoned camp. The horse divides them: Thymoetes urges it inside the walls, Capys and others want it destroyed.
  • Lesson
    Section 4 — Lines 40–49 — Laocoön’s warning
    Laocoön comes running down from the citadel to ask what madness this is — the Greeks are never more dangerous than when they are giving something away.
  • Lesson
    Section 5 — Lines 50–56 — The spear in the horse’s flank
    He hurls his spear into the belly of the horse; it sticks quivering and the hollow caverns groan. Had the gods and the Trojans’ own minds been otherwise, Troy would be standing still.
Lines 195–253 The serpents, and the horse brought in
  • Lesson
    Section 6 — Lines 195–207 — The lie believed; the serpents come
    Sinon’s trick works where ten years and a thousand ships had failed. Laocoön, chosen by lot as priest of Neptune, is sacrificing a bull when two serpents come over the calm sea from Tenedos.
  • Lesson
    Section 7 — Lines 208–219 — Laocoön and his sons seized
    They make for Laocoön, take his two small sons first, then the father himself as he comes to help — twice round his waist, twice round his neck, their heads towering above him.
  • Lesson
    Section 8 — Lines 220–233 — The death of Laocoön
    He strains at the coils, bellowing like a wounded bull that has shaken off the axe. The serpents escape to the citadel of the goddess, and the Trojans decide the priest had it coming.
  • Lesson
    Section 9 — Lines 234–245 — The horse brought in
    The walls are breached and the city laid open. Boys and unmarried girls sing round the horse as it is hauled in, four times sticking on the threshold — and four times the armour clashing inside.
  • Lesson
    Section 10 — Lines 246–253 — Cassandra; night falls
    Cassandra opens her mouth to the future and is not believed. The Trojans garland the shrines on what is to be their last day; night comes up out of the ocean, and the city sleeps.

Section 6 — Lines 195–207 — The lie believed; the serpents comeSinon’s trick works where ten years and a thousand ships had failed. Laocoön, chosen by lot as priest of Neptune, is sacrificing a bull when two serpents come over the calm sea from Tenedos.ReadSection 7 — Lines 208–219 — Laocoön and his sons seizedThey make for Laocoön, take his two small sons first, then the father himself as he comes to help — twice round his waist, twice round his neck, their heads towering above him.ReadSection 8 — Lines 220–233 — The death of LaocoönHe strains at the coils, bellowing like a wounded bull that has shaken off the axe. The serpents escape to the citadel of the goddess, and the Trojans decide the priest had it coming.ReadSection 9 — Lines 234–245 — The horse brought inThe walls are breached and the city laid open. Boys and unmarried girls sing round the horse as it is hauled in, four times sticking on the threshold — and four times the armour clashing inside.ReadSection 10 — Lines 246–253 — Cassandra; night fallsCassandra opens her mouth to the future and is not believed. Virgil, Aeneid II.1–56 and 195–253 — the set text for Verse Literature B (cycle 5, examination 2027 & 2028). 115 lines in ten sections.

Lines 57–194 are not prescribed: between the two passages Sinon talks his way inside the walls. The second passage picks up at the moment his lie is believed.

Chapter 21 (VI.21.3–5)

Psyche wavers in her resolve to kill her husband · Cupid & Psyche · GCSE Latin · OCR J282/03

1at Psyche relicta sola nisi quod infestis Furiis agitata sola non est aestu pelagi simile maerendo fluctuat,
1But Psyche, left alone, except that being harassed by the hostile Furies she is not alone, like the tide of the sea, wavers in her grieving,
2et quamvis statuto consilio et obstinato animo iam tamen facinori suas manus admovens adhuc incerta consilii titubat multis -que calamitatis suae distrahitur affectibus.
2and although having settled on a plan and (her) purpose resolute however now applying her hands to the deed still uncertain in her resolve, she falters and is torn apart by the many emotions of her misfortune.
3festinat differt, audet trepidat, diffidit irascitur; et quod est ultimum, in eodem corpore odit bestiam, diligit maritum.
3She hurries, (then) delays, dares, (then) panics, despairs, (then) rages; and, which is the final thing, in the same body she hates the beast (but) loves the husband.
4vespera tamen iam noctem trahente, praecipiti festinatione nefarii sceleris instruit apparatum.
4However, as evening now drawing on the night, in headlong haste she sets out the apparatus of the wicked crime.
5nox aderat et maritus advenerat primus -que Veneris proeliis velitatus in altum soporem descenderat.
5Night was at hand, and her husband had arrived, and, having first skirmished in the contests of love had sunk into a deep sleep.

relicta sola ... sola non estParadox
left alone ... is not alone
Apuleius plants a paradox: Psyche is relicta sola ("left alone") and yet, hounded by the Furiae, sola non est ("is not alone"). Her guilt peoples the empty room.
aestu pelagi simile ... fluctuatSimile
wavers like the swell of the sea
Her wavering is compared to the aestus pelagi, the swell of the open sea, and the verb fluctuat ("surges, billows") keeps the water-image alive. Her emotions rise and fall like waves.
festinat differt, audet trepidat, diffidit irasciturAsyndeton
she hurries, delays, dares, panics, despairs, rages
Six present-tense verbs in three clipped, conjunction-less pairs (asyndeton), each pair an opposite. The breathless rhythm enacts her flickering, contradictory feelings — we feel her mind racing.
in eodem corpore odit bestiam, diligit maritumAntithesis
in the same body she hates the beast, loves the husband
The climax of her division: in eodem corpore ("in one and the same person") she both odit and diligit. Bestiam (the monster her sisters described) is balanced against maritum (the husband she loves).
vespera ... noctem trahentePersonification
evening drawing on the night
In the ablative absolute the evening itself "drags on" the night (noctem trahente). Time is personified and made to move slowly, drawing out the suspense before the deed.
nefarii scelerisLoaded Diction
of the wicked crime
The narrator’s own judgement breaks in: the plan is a nefarium scelus, an "unspeakable crime". The heavy moral vocabulary darkens the scene just as Psyche prepares the lamp and blade.
Veneris proeliis velitatusMilitary Metaphor
having skirmished in the battles of love
Love-making is cast as warfare: Veneris proelia are the "battles of Venus", and velitatus is a technical term for the light skirmishing that opens a fight. Spent from his "campaign", Cupid sinks into deep sleep.

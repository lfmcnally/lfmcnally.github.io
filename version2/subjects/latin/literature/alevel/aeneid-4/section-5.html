56principio delubra adeunt pacemque per aras
57exquirunt; mactant lectas de more bidentes
58legiferae Cereri Phoeboque patrique Lyaeo,
59Iunoni ante omnes, cui vincla iugalia curae.
First, they go from shrine to shrine, seeking peace at every altar; they sacrifice, as custom demands, chosen sheep to Ceres the lawgiver, to Phoebus, to father Bacchus, and above all to Juno, who watches over the bonds of marriage.
60ipsa tenens dextra pateram pulcherrima Dido
61candentis vaccae media inter cornua fundit,
62aut ante ora deum pingues spatiatur ad aras,
63instauratque diem donis, pecudumque reclusis
64pectoribus inhians spirantia consulit exta.
Dido herself, loveliest of all, holds the offering-dish in her right hand and pours it between the horns of a gleaming white cow; or walks before the faces of the gods to the altars heaped with offerings, renewing the day's gifts, and, gazing into the opened breasts of the victims, searches their still-breathing entrails for a sign.
65heu, vatum ignarae mentes! quid vota furentem,
66quid delubra iuvant? est molles flamma medullas
67interea et tacitum vivit sub pectore vulnus.
But how little seers understand! What good are prayers, what good are shrines, to a woman already mad with love? All the while the flame is eating at her soft marrow, and the silent wound lives on beneath her heart.

612si tangere portus
613infandum caput ac terris adnare necesse est,
614et sic fata Iovis poscunt, hic terminus haeret,
615at bello audacis populi vexatus et armis,
'If that cursed man must reach harbour, must come to land, if this is truly fixed by the fates of Jupiter, if this is where the line is drawn — then still, let him be harried by the arms of a bold people at war,
616finibus extorris, complexu avulsus Iuli
617auxilium imploret videatque indigna suorum
618funera; nec, cum se sub leges pacis iniquae
619tradiderit, regno aut optata luce fruatur,
620sed cadat ante diem mediaque inhumatus harena.
driven from his borders, torn from Iulus's embrace, let him beg for help and watch his men die shameful deaths; and even when he has surrendered to the terms of an unjust peace, let him not enjoy his kingdom, or the life he longed for, but let him fall before his time, and lie unburied in the sand.
621haec precor, hanc vocem extremam cum sanguine fundo.
622tum vos, o Tyrii, stirpem et genus omne futurum
623exercete odiis, cinerique haec mittite nostro
624munera. nullus amor populis nec foedera sunto.
This I pray, these are the last words I pour out, with my own blood. And you, Tyrians — hunt down his line, his whole race to come, with your hatred, forever; send this as a gift to my ashes. Let there be no love between our peoples, no treaties, ever.
625exoriare aliquis nostris ex ossibus ultor
626qui face Dardanios ferroque sequare colonos,
627nunc, olim, quocumque dabunt se tempore vires,
628litora litoribus contraria, fluctibus undas
629imprecor, arma armis: pugnent ipsique nepotesque.'
Rise up out of my bones, some avenger, to come — whoever you are — and hound the settlers of Troy with fire and sword, now, or in time to come, whenever the strength arises: I call on shore to stand against shore, sea against sea, weapon against weapon — let them fight, they and their children's children after them.'

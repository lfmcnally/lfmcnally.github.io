560nate dea, potes hoc sub casu ducere somnos,
561nec quae te circum stent deinde pericula cernis,
562demens, nec Zephyros audis spirare secundos?
563illa dolos dirumque nefas in pectore versat
564certa mori, variosque irarum concitat aestus.
'Son of a goddess, can you really sleep on, with things as they stand? Can't you see, madman, what dangers are closing in around you? Can't you hear the west winds calling, fair for sailing? She is turning over treachery and dreadful violence in her heart, set on dying, and stirring up a rising storm of anger.
565non fugis hinc praeceps, dum praecipitare potestas?
566iam mare turbari trabibus saevasque videbis
567conlucere faces, iam fervere litora flammis,
568si te his attigerit terris Aurora morantem.
569heia age, rumpe moras. varium et mutabile semper
570femina.' sic fatus nocti se immiscuit atrae.
Won't you flee from here headlong, while flight is still possible? Soon you will see the sea churned up with ships, and fierce torches blazing, soon the shore ablaze with fire — if dawn finds you still lingering in this land. Come now, break off your delay. A woman is always a changeable, unpredictable thing.' With those words he vanished into the black night.
571tum vero Aeneas subitis exterritus umbris
572corripit e somno corpus sociosque fatigat
573praecipites: ' vigilate, viri, et considite transtris;
574solvite vela citi. deus aethere missus ab alto
575festinare fugam tortosque incidere funes
576ecce iterum instimulat. sequimur te, sancte deorum,
577quisquis es, imperioque iterum paremus ovantes.
578adsis o placidusque iuves et sidera caelo
579dextra feras.'
Then indeed Aeneas, terrified by the sudden vision, tore himself from sleep and roused his men, urgent: 'Wake up, men, quickly — take your places at the oars; loose the sails, and hurry. A god sent down from high heaven — look — is spurring us on again, to hasten our flight and cut the twisted ropes. We follow you, whoever you are, one of the blessed gods, and joyfully obey your command once more. Be with us, come gently to our aid, and set kind stars in the sky above us.'

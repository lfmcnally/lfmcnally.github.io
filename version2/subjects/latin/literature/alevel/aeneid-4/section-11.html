547quin morere ut merita es, ferroque averte dolorem.
548tu lacrimis evicta meis, tu prima furentem
549his, germana, malis oneras atque obicis hosti.
550non licuit thalami expertem sine crimine vitam
551degere more ferae, tales nec tangere curas;
552non servata fides cineri promissa Sychaeo.'
'No — die, as you deserve, and put an end to your grief with the sword. You, sister, gave way to my tears, and it was you who first loaded me down, mad as I already was, with all this suffering, and threw me to my enemy. I was not allowed to live out my life, innocent, untouched by marriage, the way a wild creature does, free of troubles like these. The vow I made to Sychaeus's ashes — I have not kept it.'
553tantos illa suo rumpebat pectore questus:
554Aeneas celsa in puppi iam certus eundi
555carpebat somnos rebus iam rite paratis.
Such were the great laments that burst from her heart. Meanwhile Aeneas, now determined to sail, lay on the high stern enjoying his sleep, everything already properly prepared.
556huic se forma dei vultu redeuntis eodem
557obtulit in somnis rursusque ita visa monere est,
558omnia Mercurio similis, vocemque coloremque
559et crines flavos et membra decora iuventae:
In his dreams the shape of the god appeared to him again, returning with the same face as before, and seemed to warn him once more, just as before — in every way like Mercury, his voice, his colouring, his golden hair, and the graceful limbs of youth:

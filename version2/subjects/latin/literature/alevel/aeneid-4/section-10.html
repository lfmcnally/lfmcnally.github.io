529at non infelix animi Phoenissa neque umquam
530solvitur in somnos oculisve aut pectore noctem
531accipit: ingeminant curae rursusque resurgens
532saevit amor magnoque irarum fluctuat aestu.
533sic adeo insistit secumque ita corde volutat:
But the unhappy queen of Carthage, sick at heart, finds no rest, no release into sleep; night brings no peace to her eyes or to her breast. Her anxieties redouble, and her love, rising up again, rages and surges on a great swell of anger. So she keeps returning to it, turning it over in her heart:
534en, quid ago? rursusne procos inrisa priores
535experiar, Nomadumque petam conubia supplex,
536quos ego sim totiens iam dedignata maritos?
537Iliacas igitur classes atque ultima Teucrum
538iussa sequar? quiane auxilio iuvat ante levatos
539et bene apud memores veteris stat gratia facti?
'Well then, what am I to do? Should I go crawling back to my old suitors, only to be laughed at all over again, and beg the Africans, humbled, for marriage — men I have already refused so many times as husbands? Or should I follow the Trojan fleet instead, and obey their commanders' every last word? Because, of course, they're so grateful for the help I gave them before, and the memory of that old kindness runs so deep!
540quis me autem, fac velle, sinet ratibusve superbis
541invisam accipiet? nescis heu, perdita, necdum
542Laomedonteae sentis periuria gentis?
543quid tum? sola fuga nautas comitabor ovantes?
But even if I wanted to — who would let me? Who would take a woman they despise aboard their proud ships? Don't you understand, you fool, ruined as you are — don't you see yet the treachery bred in that Trojan blood? What then? Should I run off alone, and join their crews as they sail away rejoicing?
544an Tyriis omnique manu stipata meorum
545inferar et, quos Sidonia vix urbe revelli,
546rursus agam pelago et ventis dare vela iubebo?'
Or should I go after them with all the Tyrians around me, my whole people gathered close — the very people I could hardly tear away from Tyre in the first place — and order them to set sail again, back out onto the sea and into the wind?'

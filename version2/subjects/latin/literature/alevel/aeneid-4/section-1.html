1at regina gravi iamdudum saucia cura
2vulnus alit venis et caeco carpitur igni.
3multa viri virtus animo multusque recursat
4gentis honos: haerent infixi pectore vultus
5verbaque, nec placidam membris dat cura quietem.
But the queen had long since been wounded by a deep anxiety; she feeds the wound in her veins, and is consumed by a fire she cannot see. Again and again his courage comes back to her mind, again and again the honour of his line; his face, his words, stay fixed in her heart, and her love allows her limbs no peaceful rest.
6postera Phoebea lustrabat lampade terras
7umentemque Aurora polo dimoverat umbram,
8cum sic unanimam adloquitur male sana sororem:
9Anna soror, quae me suspensam insomnia terrent!
The next dawn was lighting the earth with Phoebus's lamp, and had cleared the dewy shadow from the sky, when, hardly herself, she spoke to her sister, who shared her every thought: 'Anna, my sister, what dreams keep me in such suspense, and terrify me!
10quis novus hic nostris successit sedibus hospes,
11quem sese ore ferens, quam forti pectore et armis!
12credo equidem, nec vana fides, genus esse deorum.
13degeneres animos timor arguit. heu, quibus ille
14iactatus fatis! quae bella exhausta canebat!'
What a stranger this is who has come to stay in our home — how noble his bearing, how brave his heart, how strong in arms! I really do believe — and it is no idle faith — that his family is divine. Fear betrays a coward's spirit. Ah, what fates he has been driven through! What wars he told of, and endured to the end!'

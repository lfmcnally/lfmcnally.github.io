45dis equidem auspicibus reor et Iunone secunda
46hunc cursum Iliacas vento tenuisse carinas.
47quam tu urbem, soror, hanc cernes, quae surgere regna
48coniugio tali! Teucrum comitantibus armis
49Punica se quantis attollet gloria rebus!
'It was surely with the gods as our protectors, and Juno's favour, that the Trojan ships held this course before the wind. What a city you will see rise here, sister — what a kingdom, from such a marriage! With the Trojans' arms marching beside our own, to what heights will Carthage's glory raise itself!
50tu modo posce deos veniam, sacrisque litatis
51indulge hospitio causasque innecte morandi,
52dum pelago desaevit hiems et aquosus Orion,
53quassataeque rates, dum non tractabile caelum.'
You need only ask the gods' pardon, and once the offerings have won their favour, give yourself over to hospitality, and weave reasons to keep him here — while winter still rages at sea, and rainy Orion, while the ships still lie battered, and the sky refuses safe passage.'
54his dictis impenso animum flammavit amore
55spemque dedit dubiae menti solvitque pudorem.
With these words she set Dido's heart ablaze with a passion beyond reason, gave hope to her wavering mind, and dissolved what remained of her shame.

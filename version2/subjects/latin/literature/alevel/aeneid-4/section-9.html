Questions to think about · 3
  1. Juno's plan is set out in great detail (117–127). What does the level of detail tell us about her as a planner, and about how little Dido and Aeneas control their own lives?
  2. Is the "marriage" in the cave a real marriage? Look closely at Juno's words in 126–127 and compare them with what you know happens later.
  3. Why does Venus smile at the end of the section, and what does her smile suggest about the outcome?
The cave and the storm background

The plan Juno sets out here is carried out in lines 129–172, which are not part of the set text. Dido and Aeneas ride out to the hunt in great splendour; the storm breaks; everyone runs for shelter; and the two of them find themselves alone in the same cave. Earth and Juno give the signal, lightning flashes like wedding torches, and nymphs cry out on the mountain tops.

Virgil comments that this day was the first cause of death and of evils. Dido calls it a marriage, but Aeneas never does, and the difference between them on this point lies at the heart of their later quarrel.

ContextAccording to tradition, Hannibal kept this poison hidden inside a ring on his finger — meaning he had, quite literally, been carrying his own way out with him for years, just in case it ever came to this.
Stylememor pristinarum virtutum 'remembering his past deeds of courage': Nepos gives Hannibal control right up to the final word of the chapter. Rather than being executed or captured, Hannibal chooses his own ending — the last, and arguably greatest, act of his own agency in the whole Life.
1–3
While these things were taking place in Asia, it happened by chance that in Rome envoys of Prusias were dining with Titus Quinctius Flamininus, an ex-consul, and there, when Hannibal was mentioned, one of the envoys said that he was in the kingdom of Prusias.
4
The next day Flamininus reported this to the senate.
4–5
The senators, who thought that while Hannibal lived they would never be free from plots, sent envoys to Bithynia, among them Flamininus,
6–7
to ask the king not to keep their bitterest enemy with him, but to hand him over to them. Prusias did not dare say no to them:
8
he did, however, refuse this: that they should not ask him to do anything which would go against the laws of hospitality:
8–9
they themselves, if they could, should arrest him; they would easily find the place where he was.
9–10
For Hannibal kept himself in one place, in a small fort which had been given to him by the king as a gift,
10–11
and he had built it in such a way that he had exits in every part of the building, clearly fearing that it might actually come to that, which is exactly what happened.
12–13
When the Roman envoys had come to this place and surrounded his house with a large force, a boy looking out from the door told Hannibal that more armed men than usual were appearing.
13–15
Hannibal ordered him to go round all the doors of the building and quickly report back to him whether he was surrounded in the same way on every side.
16–18
When the boy had quickly reported what the situation was and shown that all the exits were guarded, Hannibal realised that this had not happened by chance, but that he was the one being hunted, and that his life could no longer be preserved.
18–19
So that he would not lose it by someone else's decision, remembering his past deeds of courage, he took the poison which he had always been in the habit of keeping with him.

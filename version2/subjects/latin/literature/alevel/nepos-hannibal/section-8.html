Grammarapparando a gerund.
Vocabularybellum ... composuerunt bellum compono is a set phrase meaning 'I make peace'.
ContextNepos doesn't give any detail here, but the terms were brutal: Carthage's empire was shrunk to just Africa, its navy cut to ten ships, and it had to pay 200 talents (over 5 tonnes) of silver every year for 50 years — and even had to ask Rome's permission before making war on anyone. In 149 BC the Carthaginians finally rebelled against these terms, starting the Third Punic War, which ended with Carthage being wiped out completely.

Grammarhaberent refers to the Carthaginians keeping Hannibal in command; the clauses inside the reported speech (rogarent, foret, haberent) are subjunctive, as expected in indirect statement.
Asyndetonmunus eorum gratum acceptumque esse; obsides ... futuros; captivos non remissuros — the senate answers the Carthaginians' three requests one after another with no connecting word, giving a blunt, no-nonsense effect: they know exactly what they will and won't grant.

Vocabularyrex ... reges Nepos's use of 'king' here is really his Roman shorthand for the two Carthaginian officials properly called suffetes, more like 'judges' than kings.
Vocabularybini is the special Latin word used for things that naturally come in pairs, like these two annually-elected officials.
Polyptotonquotannis annui bini three words in a row all built around the same idea of 'every year, yearly, in pairs': repeating a root idea like this in slightly different grammatical forms is polyptoton, and it hammers home just how regular and formal this Carthaginian system was.
1–2
While he had been very busily engaged in preparations, the Carthaginians made peace with the Romans.
2–3
Hannibal, nevertheless, afterwards was in command of the army and carried on war in Africa, and so did his brother Mago, right up until Publius Sulpicius and Gaius Aurelius were consuls.
4–6
For while these men were magistrates, Carthaginian envoys came to Rome, to give thanks to the senate and Roman people because they had made peace with them, and on account of this to present them with a golden crown,
6
and at the same time to ask that their hostages might live at Fregellae and that their prisoners should be returned.
7–8
To them, in accordance with a decree of the senate, the reply was given: that their gift was pleasing and accepted; that the hostages would be where they had requested;
8–10
that they would not return the prisoners, because they were still keeping in charge of the army Hannibal, a man very hostile to the Roman name, through whose efforts the war had been undertaken, and likewise his brother Mago.
11
When this reply was known, the Carthaginians recalled Hannibal and Mago home.
11–12
On his return here, Hannibal was made a king, in the twenty-second year after he had been praetor.
12–13
For, as is true of the consuls at Rome, so at Carthage a pair of kings was created annually every year.

Grammarconservatis suis rebus is an ablative absolute referring to Hannibal's money; illisis Cretensibus omnibus is a second ablative absolute straight after it, with no et joining them (asyndeton).
VocabularyPoenus 'the Carthaginian': using this label instead of Hannibal's actual name is antonomasia (substituting a description for a proper name), and it underlines here that Hannibal is, first and foremost, Rome's enemy.
ContextPrusias I was King of Bithynia, on the south coast of the Black Sea (sometimes called Pontus), roughly modern North Turkey. He was a ruthless military leader — on one occasion he had women and children put to death and let his soldiers loot freely — but he did at least grant Hannibal asylum, even while staying neutral in Rome's war against Antiochus.

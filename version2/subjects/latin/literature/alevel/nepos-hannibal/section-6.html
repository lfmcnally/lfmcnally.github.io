ContextMarcellus was a highly decorated general, one of the few to push Hannibal back (at Nola in Campania), and is most famous for capturing Syracuse in Sicily in 212 BC — the siege in which the scientist Archimedes was killed. He himself died later, in 208 BC.

Anaphoranemo ... nemo starting both halves of the sentence with the same word (nemo, 'no one') hammers home just how completely dominant Hannibal was.
Asyndetonnemo ei in acie restitit, nemo adversus eum ... in campo castra posuit — the two halves are simply placed side by side with no et joining them, which makes the point land harder and faster.
AlliterationThe whole sentence rings with p and c sounds (post, Cannensem, pugnam, campo, castra, posuit) — a genuinely accomplished piece of writing, even if Nepos doesn't sustain this level of polish consistently throughout the Life.
Contextin campo Hannibal's victories mostly came on open ground; the Romans generally did better fighting him in the hills, where his cavalry (his real advantage) counted for less.
1
After this battle was fought, Hannibal set out for Rome without any resistance.
He halted in the hills near the city.
2–3
After he had had his camp there for several days and was returning to Capua, the Roman dictator Quintus Fabius Maximus opposed him in Falernian territory.
4–5
But Hannibal, although hemmed in by the narrowness of the places, got himself out by night without any loss to his army, and tricked Fabius, a most skilful general.

Dating events in the ancient world background

The Romans had no single numbered calendar of years. They identified a year by the names of the two consuls who held office in it, so that to say when something happened a writer named the consuls of that year. Lists of consuls (the fasti) were kept, and historians and chronographers worked from them.

Writers did not always agree, because their sources differed and because it was easy to attach an event to the wrong pair of names. Here Nepos sets three authorities side by side, giving dates of 183, 182 and 181 BC, and does not choose between them. This kind of care over sources is typical of him: he was himself a chronographer, and his friend Atticus had written a compact chronology of Roman history, the Liber Annalis.

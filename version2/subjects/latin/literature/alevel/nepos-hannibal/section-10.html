Grammartam ... quam are correlatives, 'as ... as'; si ... voluisset is a conditional clause; eius refers to Hannibal.
Grammarin gerendo bello and in suscipiendo (bello) are both gerundives, literally 'in the war being waged' / 'in the war being undertaken'.
Balancesi tam in gerendo bello consiliis eius parere voluisset, quam in suscipiendo instituerat — the sentence sets Antiochus's early enthusiasm (suscipiendo, 'undertaking' the war) against his later reluctance to actually follow advice (gerendo, 'waging' it): all talk, not enough follow-through.
Contextpropius Tiberi the Tiber is the river running through Rome, i.e. Nepos is saying Antiochus could have fought Rome on Rome's own doorstep. Instead he was beaten at Thermopylae, in central Greece, in 191 BC, then defeated again the following year after retreating into Asia.

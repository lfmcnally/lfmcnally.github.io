Nepos sets out both versions side by side and does not choose, like a careful researcher.

The diminutive is contemptuous: a famous commander dies at the hands of mere slaves, a humiliating end.

A past unreal condition imagines what might have been, as in chapter 1. If Antiochus had taken Hannibal's advice, the war would have been fought in Italy.

as much in waging the war as in undertaking it
The matching gerunds contrast the king who took Hannibal's advice to start the war with the king who ignored it while fighting.

nearer the Tiber than at Thermopylae
Rome's own river is set against a pass in distant Greece, which shows how close Rome came to real danger.

Many foolish schemes are set against not a single desertion, which contrasts the foolish king with his loyal adviser.
paucis navibusEmphasis
a few ships
Hannibal is given only a small command, which suggests that Antiochus undervalued him.
ipse … fuit superiorEcho

Ipse stresses Hannibal's personal success in a lost battle, and superior echoes chapter 1, where he always came away the victor.
Notes on the text 4
CyrenaeorumCyrene
of the Cyrenaeans
Cyrene was a Greek city in Libya, east of Carthaginian territory, a safe place from which to contact Carthage.
de Magonis interituMago's death
about Mago's death
Neither of Nepos's versions matches Livy, who says Mago died in 203 BC of a wound on the way home from Italy.
ThermopylisThermopylae
at Thermopylae
The narrow pass in central Greece where Antiochus was defeated by the Romans under Manius Acilius Glabrio in 191 BC, after which he withdrew to Asia.
Rhodiorum classem in Pamphylio mariThe battle off Pamphylia
the Rhodian fleet in the Pamphylian Sea
The battle of the Eurymedon, off Side in southern Asia Minor, in 190 BC. Rhodes, an ally of Rome, defeated the king's fleet, though the wing Hannibal commanded held its own.
Questions to think about · 3
  1. How does Nepos show Hannibal's loyalty to Antiochus despite the king's mistakes?
  2. "propius Tiberi quam in Thermopylis": how does Nepos use geography to make his point about what might have been?
  3. Nepos reports two versions of Mago's death and does not choose between them. What does this suggest about him as a biographer?
Hannibal and Antiochus background

Antiochus III welcomed Hannibal in 195 BC. According to Livy, Hannibal urged him to carry the war to Italy, with Carthage brought in on his side, rather than fight Rome in Greece. Antiochus chose Greece: he landed there in 192 BC, was beaten by the Romans at Thermopylae in 191, and withdrew to Asia.

In 190 BC Hannibal commanded part of the king's fleet and was defeated by the Rhodians off Side in Pamphylia (the battle of the Eurymedon). Later that year, or early in 189, the Romans under Lucius Scipio crushed Antiochus' army at Magnesia. By the peace of Apamea (188 BC) Antiochus gave up his lands in Asia Minor and was required to hand Hannibal over to Rome.

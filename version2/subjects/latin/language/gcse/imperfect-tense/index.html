The imperfect tense

portabam, "I was carrying" or "I used to carry": the first past tense, for something going on, or happening again and again.
Try it →
Lesson 3.1The imperfect and adjectives

A tense for the past

The imperfect tense describes something that was going on in the past, or that used to happen again and again. English usually needs two or three words for it: "was carrying", "used to carry".

servus cibum portabat.
The slave was carrying food.
-ba- marks the imperfect, and -t is still "he" or "she".
the sign of the imperfectthe person ending
  • The sign of the imperfect is -ba-, between the stem and the person ending.
  • The person endings are the ones you know, except that "I" ends in -m: portabam.
  • Translate it "was ...ing" or "were ...ing" for something going on, and "used to ..." for a habit.

The 3rd and 4th conjugations put an e before the -ba-: trahebam, audiebam. sum has no -ba- at all, but its person endings are the same: eram, erat, erant.

Translating the imperfect

  • Find the -ba-, or er- in sum, then the person ending.
  • Something going on: "was ...ing" or "were ...ing". servi laborabant, "the slaves were working".
  • A habit: "used to ...". semper laborabant, "they always used to work".
  • erat is "he was", "she was" or "there was"; erant is "they were" or "there were".
nautae diu navigabant.
The sailors were sailing for a long time.
domina Romae habitabat.
The mistress used to live in Rome.
servi in foro erant.
The slaves were in the forum.
erant is the imperfect of sum: "they were".

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • -bam is "I": portabam, "I was carrying". "He was carrying" is portabat.
  • -bat is one person, -bant more than one: check the last letters, as in the present.
  • erat is "he was"; est is "he is". The two look alike.
  • The imperfect is past. "Is carrying" is the present; portabat is "was carrying".

Adverbs

laete, "happily": words that say how, when or how long something is done, and how to make them from adjectives.
Try it →
Lesson 3.3The imperfect and adjectives

What an adverb does

An adjective describes a noun. An adverb describes a verb: it says how, when or how long something is done. "He works happily"; "they arrived at last".

servus laete laborat.
The slave works happily.
laete comes from laetus, happy.
the adverbthe verb it describes
  • To make an adverb from a 2-1-2 adjective, take the stem and add -e: laetus, laete.
  • Take the stem from the feminine: misera gives misere, and pulchra gives pulchre.
  • An adverb never changes its ending.

Making adverbs

AdjectiveAdverbEnglish
laetus, happylaetehappily
iratus, angryirateangrily
stultus, foolishstultefoolishly, stupidly
miser, misera, wretchedmiserewretchedly, miserably
pulcher, pulchra, beautifulpulchrebeautifully
Other words end in -e too, such as the infinitive monere. When the word is built on an adjective you know, it is an adverb.

Five adverbs to learn

These five are on the Chapter 3 list as they are. Most say when or how long.

LatinEnglish
deindethen, next
diufor a long time
statimat once, immediately
subitosuddenly
tandemat last, finally
nuntius statim currit.
The messenger runs at once.
servi diu laborabant.
The slaves were working for a long time.
diu answers "how long?", like the accusative of time.

Translating them

  • An adverb usually comes just before the verb it describes.
  • In English it can go before or after the verb: "he angrily shouts", "he shouts angrily".
  • subito, tandem and deinde often sound best at the start of the English: "At last the slaves arrive."
subito nautae discedunt.
Suddenly the sailors leave.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • laete is an adverb, "happily". laeta is an adjective, "happy", and needs a noun to agree with.
  • An adverb never changes: irate is the same with servus or servi.
  • diu is "for a long time": how long something went on.
  • deinde is "then" in the sense of "next".

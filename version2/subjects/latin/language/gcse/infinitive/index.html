The infinitive and iubeo

portare, "to carry": the infinitive, and how iubeo uses it to give an order.
Try it →
Lesson 2.5Cases and conjugations

What the infinitive is

The infinitive is the "to" form of a verb: "to carry", "to see". English uses two words for it. Latin uses one, and it nearly always ends in -re.

dominus servum laborare iubet.
The master orders the slave to work.
laborare is the infinitive: "to work". iubet is "orders".
the infinitivethe verb it goes with
  • The infinitive does not change for the person: portare is always "to carry".
  • It is the second form given in the word list: porto, portare.
  • sum is irregular: its infinitive is esse, "to be".

The four infinitives

ConjugationI ...InfinitiveEnglish
1stportoportareto carry
2ndmoneomonereto warn
3rdtrahotrahereto drag
4thaudioaudireto hear
irregularsumesseto be
The 2nd and 3rd conjugation infinitives look alike. In monere the first e is long; in trahere it is short. When you translate, it makes no difference: both are "to" + the verb.

Orders with iubeo

iubeo means "I order". The person given the order goes in the accusative, and what they are to do goes in the infinitive. English works the same way: "he orders the slave to work".

dominus servos aquam portare iubet.
The master orders the slaves to carry water.
Two accusatives: servos are the people ordered, and aquam is what they carry.
nuntius pueros venire iubet.
The messenger orders the boys to come.

Translating an order

  • Find iubet or iubent and the subject: who gives the order?
  • Then the accusative: who is ordered? Put them straight after "orders".
  • Then the infinitive with "to", and anything that goes with it: "to carry water".

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • portare is "to carry". An infinitive has no person: "he carries" is portat.
  • After iubeo, the accusative is the person ordered: servum laborare iubet is "orders the slave to work".
  • monere and trahere look alike, but the English is still just "to warn", "to drag".
  • "Tell" is a good translation of iubeo in an order: "he tells the slave to work".

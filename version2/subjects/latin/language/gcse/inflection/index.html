Inflection and person endings

Latin shows who is doing what by changing the ends of its words. On a verb, the ending tells you who is doing it.
Try it →
Lesson 1.1Foundations

What inflection is

English mostly uses word order to show who is doing what: "the dog bites the man" means something different from "the man bites the dog". Latin uses the ends of its words instead. Changing the ending of a word to change its job is called inflection.

English still does this a little. "I" becomes "me", "he" becomes "him", and "walk" becomes "walks" after "he". Latin does it with almost every word, so the ending is the first thing to look at.

ambulat.
He walks, or she walks, or it walks.
ambula- means "walk". The ending -t says who: he, she or it. One Latin word, two English words.
the stem: what the verb meansthe ending: who is doing it
  • A Latin verb has a stem, which carries the meaning, and an ending, which tells you who is doing it.
  • Latin does not need a separate word for "I", "you", "he" or "they": the ending already says it.
  • So one Latin verb is often two words in English: clamo, "I shout".

The six person endings

There are six endings, one for each person. Every tense you meet later uses the same set, so they are worth knowing by heart.

PersonEndingEnglishExample
singular1st-oIclamo: I shout
2nd-syou (one person)clamas: you shout
3rd-the, she, itclamat: he shouts
plural1st-musweclamamus: we shout
2nd-tisyou (more than one)clamatis: you shout
3rd-nttheyclamant: they shout
Say them as one word until they stick: o, s, t, mus, tis, nt. In the first person, the -o takes the place of the stem's last letter: clama- + o is clamo.
laboramus.
We work.
-mus is "we".

Translating a verb on its own

  • Find the ending first and turn it into the English person: -nt, "they".
  • Then add the meaning from the stem: navigant, "they sail".
  • -t can be "he", "she" or "it". On its own any of them is right; in a story, the sentence tells you which.
  • English has one "you" where Latin has two: -s for one person, -tis for more than one. Both are "you" in English.
vocant.
They call.
-nt is "they", and voca- is "call".

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • -t is one person and -nt is more than one. "They" for clamat loses the mark.
  • -s and -tis are both "you". Do not mix -tis up with -mus, "we".
  • There is no separate Latin word for "I" or "he" here: the ending already contains it.

2-1-2 adjectives and agreement

laetus, laeta, laetum: adjectives that borrow the endings of the 2nd, 1st and 2nd declensions, and always match their noun.
Try it →
Lesson 3.2The imperfect and adjectives

What an adjective does

An adjective describes a noun: "a happy slave", "a big house". In Latin it changes its ending to match the noun it describes. This is called agreement.

servus laetus laborat.
The happy slave is working.
laetus is masculine, singular and nominative, like servus.
the nounthe adjective that agrees with it
domina villam magnam habet.
The mistress has a big house.
magnam is feminine, singular and accusative, like villam.
  • An adjective agrees with its noun in gender, number and case.
  • In Latin the adjective usually comes after its noun. In English it goes before: servus laetus, "the happy slave".
  • Adjectives of size and number often come first: multi servi, "many slaves".

The endings

They are called 2-1-2 adjectives because the masculine goes like servus (2nd declension), the feminine like puella (1st) and the neuter like templum (2nd).

Matching, with different endings

Agreement is about gender, number and case. The endings often look alike, but they need not. nauta, sailor, is 1st declension but masculine, so the adjective that goes with it is masculine.

nauta laetus navigat.
The happy sailor is sailing.
nauta ends in -a, but a sailor is a man, so laetus is masculine.
servus vinum bonum portat.
The slave carries good wine.
vinum is neuter, so bonum is neuter: accusative singular.

Translating them

  • Find the noun, then the adjective that matches it in gender, number and case.
  • Put the English adjective before the noun: "the angry master".
  • An adjective after est or erat describes the subject: dominus iratus erat, "the master was angry".
  • A plural adjective on its own can stand for people: multi, "many people"; Romani, "the Romans".
dominus iratus erat.
The master was angry.
servus dominum iratum timebat.
The slave was afraid of the angry master.
iratum is accusative, so it goes with dominum: the master is the angry one.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • An adjective can have a different ending from its noun and still agree: nauta laetus.
  • -a on an adjective can be feminine singular or neuter plural: multa verba, "many words".
  • With two nouns, match the adjective by its case: in servus dominum iratum timebat, the master is the angry one.
  • English puts the adjective first: puella pulchra is "the beautiful girl".

Neuter nouns

Latin nouns are masculine, feminine or neuter. The neuter nouns of the 2nd declension end in -um, and they have one rule worth knowing: the nominative and the accusative are always the same.

servus vinum portat.
The slave carries the wine.
vinum is the object here.
nominative: the subjectaccusative: the object
vinum in taberna est.
The wine is in the shop.
The same word, vinum, is the subject here. Only the sense of the sentence shows which.
  • A neuter noun has the same ending for the subject and the object: -um in the singular, -a in the plural.
  • Its other cases go like servus: templi, templo, templorum, templis.
  • arma, "weapons", is only ever plural.

The Chapter 2 neuter nouns are auxilium, help; bellum, war; donum, gift; periculum, danger; templum, temple; verbum, word; vinum, wine; and the plural arma, weapons.

vir, puer, ager, liber

Four masculine nouns end in -r in the nominative singular. After that they go like servus. puer keeps its e; ager and liber drop it.

liber, book, goes like ager: librum, libri. The word list shows which kind a noun is by giving the genitive: puer, pueri but liber, libri.

Translating them

  • A neuter noun in -um or -a could be the subject or the object. Find the verb, then ask which makes sense.
  • An -a ending can be a neuter plural: dona is "gifts", templa is "temples".
  • A neuter plural subject still has a plural verb: arma sunt, "there are weapons".
pueri dona portant.
The boys carry the gifts.
dona ends in -a, but it is the neuter plural of donum, and the object.
viri arma parant.
The men prepare the weapons.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • -a on a neuter noun is plural: templa is "temples". One temple is templum.
  • A neuter subject and a neuter object look alike. Use the verb and the sense to decide.
  • vir is "man", and viri can be "of the man" or "the men".
  • ager and liber lose their e: agrum, libros.

Lesson 2.4Cases and conjugations

Four families of verbs

Every verb in Chapter 1 went like porto. Latin has four families of verbs, called conjugations. They all use the person endings you already know. What changes is the vowel in front of the ending.

servus dominum monet.
The slave warns the master.
-t is still "he" or "she". The e shows that moneo is 2nd conjugation.
the stem: what the verb meansthe ending: who is doing it
  • 1st conjugation verbs have a: portas. 2nd have e: mones.
  • 3rd conjugation verbs have i, and u in "they": trahis, trahunt.
  • 4th conjugation verbs keep an i all the way through: audis, audiunt.
  • Whatever the vowel, the last letters still tell you the person.

Translating them

  • Find the ending first, as before: -t is "he", "she" or "it"; -mus is "we".
  • -unt and -iunt mean "they", just like -nt.
  • Only the spelling changes with the conjugation: trahit is "he drags", just as portat is "he carries".
pueri epistulas scribunt.
The boys are writing letters.
-unt is "they": 3rd conjugation.
ancillae in villa dormiunt.
The slave-girls are sleeping in the house.
-iunt is "they": 4th conjugation.
dominus servum punit.
The master punishes the slave.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • -unt and -iunt are "they": ducunt, "they lead"; veniunt, "they come".
  • -es in the 2nd conjugation is "you": vides, "you see". "He sees" is videt.
  • The first person of the 2nd and 4th ends in -eo and -io: video is "I see", and audio is "I hear".
  • A 3rd conjugation -it is "he", "she" or "it": legit, "he reads".

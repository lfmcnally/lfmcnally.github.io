The genitive, dative and ablative

Three more cases, each with its own English: the genitive for "of", the dative for "to" or "for", and the ablative for "by", "with" or "from".
Try it →
Lesson 2.1Cases and conjugations

Three new cases

In Chapter 1 a noun was either the subject (nominative) or the object (accusative). Latin has three more cases, and each one carries a little English word with it: "of", "to" or "with". Find the case, and you know which word to add.

servus pecuniam domini portat.
The slave carries the master's money.
domini is genitive: "of the master", or "the master's". It belongs to pecuniam.
the noun in the new casethe word it goes with
dominus servo cibum dat.
The master gives food to the slave.
servo is dative: "to the slave". The dative goes naturally with giving.
servus equum gladio necat.
The slave kills the horse with a sword.
gladio is ablative: "with a sword", the thing he uses.
  • The genitive means "of": pecunia domini, "the money of the master", or "the master's money".
  • The dative means "to" or "for": servo dat, "gives to the slave".
  • The ablative on its own usually means "with" or "by": the thing someone uses, as in gladio, "with a sword".

The endings

Here are all five cases of puella and servus. The new Chapter 2 nouns aqua, hora and taberna go like puella; annus and murus go like servus.

The genitive plural is the easiest to spot: -arum and -orum always mean "of" more than one. The dative and ablative plural are always the same: -is.

Working out which case

Some endings do more than one job, so let the rest of the sentence decide.

  • -ae can be "of the girl", "to the girl" or "the girls". If the verb is plural and there is no other subject, it is the girls.
  • -o on a 2nd declension noun is dative or ablative: "to the slave" or "with the sword". A person is usually dative; a thing is usually ablative.
  • A genitive usually sits next to the noun it belongs to, often just after it.
  • A verb of giving usually has a dative: who is it given to?
feminae cibum servis dant.
The women give food to the slaves.
dant is plural, so feminae is the subject: "the women". servis is dative, after a verb of giving.
amici epistulam nuntii portant.
The friends carry the messenger's letter.
nuntii sits next to epistulam: "the letter of the messenger".

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • -ae has three possible jobs: "of the girl", "to the girl" or "the girls". Check the verb before you choose.
  • -o on a 2nd declension noun means "to", "for", "with" or "by". The subject would end in -us.
  • -is is dative or ablative plural: servis is "to the slaves" or "with the slaves". The subject would be servi.
  • The genitive belongs to another noun. In servus pecuniam domini portat, the master owns the money and the slave carries it.

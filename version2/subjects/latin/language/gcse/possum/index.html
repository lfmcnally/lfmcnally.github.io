possum

possum, potes, potest: "I can", "I am able", with an infinitive. And debeo, "I must", which works in the same way.
Try it →
Lesson 3.5The imperfect and adjectives

What possum is

possum means "I can" or "I am able". It is sum with pot- in front. Like "can" in English, it needs another verb to finish it: an infinitive.

servus currere non potest.
The slave cannot run.
potest is "he can" and currere is "to run". After "can", English drops the "to".
possum or debeothe infinitive
  • Where sum begins with s, possum has pos-: possum, possumus, possunt.
  • Where sum begins with e, possum has pot-: potes, potest, potestis.
  • The infinitive usually comes just before it.

The imperfect is pot- with the imperfect of sum: poteram, poterat. Translate it "could" or "was able to". The infinitive is posse, "to be able".

debeo with an infinitive

debeo means "I owe". With an infinitive it means "I must", "I ought to" or "I should". It is a regular 2nd conjugation verb: debes, debet, debent.

servi laborare debent.
The slaves must work.
nautae discedere debebant.
The sailors had to leave.
debebant is imperfect: "had to" is the clearest English.

Translating them

  • Find possum or debeo, then the infinitive that goes with it.
  • With possum, drop the "to": "he can run". Or keep it with "able": "he is able to run".
  • non with possum is "cannot" or "was not able to".
  • With debeo: "must", "ought to", "should", or "had to" in the imperfect.
puella dormire non poterat.
The girl could not sleep.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • potest is "he can"; possunt is "they can". Check the ending as you would with sum.
  • poterat is past: "he could", "he was able".
  • Keep the infinitive with its verb: currere potest, "he can run".
  • debeo on its own is "I owe". With an infinitive it is "I must".

The present tense

porto, portas, portat: the 1st conjugation and its six endings, and the three ways to say it in English.
Try it →
Lesson 1.2Foundations

What the present tense is

The present tense says what is happening now, or what happens regularly. In Latin it is one word: the stem of the verb with a person ending on it.

portamus.
We carry, or we are carrying.
porta- is the stem, "carry". -mus is "we".
the stemthe ending
  • Every verb in Chapter 1 belongs to the 1st conjugation: its stem ends in -a.
  • The stem stays the same; only the ending changes.
  • The one exception is "I": the -o takes the place of the stem's a, so porta- + o is porto.

The pattern: porto

PersonLatinEnglish
IportoI carry
you (one)portasyou carry
he, she, itportathe carries
weportamuswe carry
you (more than one)portatisyou carry
theyportantthey carry

Three ways to say it in English

English has three present tenses where Latin has one. All three are correct translations; use the one that sounds natural.

LatinSimpleContinuousWith "do"
pugnantthey fightthey are fightingthey do fight
vocoI callI am callingI do call
necat.
He kills, or he is killing.
"Kills" needs its s in English after "he" or "she".
The "do" form is rare in a straight translation. It comes into its own with "not", which is in lesson 1.4.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • Make the English agree: "he carries", not "he carry".
  • -t is "he", "she" or "it". Any of them is right on its own; the mark is for one person.
  • amo can be "I love" or "I like". Both are on the GCSE list.

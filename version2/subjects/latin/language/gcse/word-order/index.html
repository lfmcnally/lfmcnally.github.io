Word order and the five important words

Where Latin puts its words, how to put them back into English, and five words you will meet in almost every sentence: et, sed, non, nunc, semper.
Try it →
Lesson 1.4Foundations

Latin word order

A Latin sentence usually runs subject, object, verb, with the verb last. English runs subject, verb, object. So when you translate, the verb moves.

femina epistulam portat.
The woman carries the letter.
Latin order: woman, letter, carries. English order: the woman carries the letter.
the subjectthe verb
  • The verb usually comes last. Read to the end of the sentence before you start writing.
  • The endings, not the order, decide who does what. A writer can put a word first to stress it: epistulam femina portat still means "the woman carries the letter".
  • Latin has no "the" or "a", and English sometimes needs "his" or "her" where Latin has nothing: dominus servum vocat can be "the master calls his slave".

The five important words

LatinEnglishWhere it goes
etandbetween the two things it joins
sedbutat the start of the second idea
nonnotjust before the verb
nuncnowanywhere, often first
semperalwaysanywhere, often just before the verb
puellae non laborant.
The girls are not working.
non sits just before the verb. English needs "do not" or "are not": "the girls do not work".
dominus nunc clamat, sed servi semper laborant.
The master is shouting now, but the slaves are always working.
sed starts the second idea, with a new subject and a new verb.

Putting it into English

  • Find the verb at the end, and the subject in the nominative.
  • Write the subject, then the verb, then the object.
  • Put "not", "now" and "always" where English wants them: "the slave does not work", "the slave is now working", "the slave always works".
  • Two subjects joined by et take a plural verb: servus et ancilla laborant, "the slave and the slave-girl are working".
ancilla cibum non parat.
The slave-girl is not preparing the food.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • Do not start writing until you have found the verb.
  • non belongs to the verb after it, not to the noun before it.
  • After sed or et, look for a new verb: a new part of the sentence has started.
  • nunc is "now" and semper is "always": they are easy to swap under pressure.

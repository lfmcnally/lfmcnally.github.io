The locative, and time how long

Romae, "at Rome"; quinque horas, "for five hours": a city or a length of time with no preposition at all.
Try it →
Lesson 2.7Cases and conjugations

Cities: at, to and from

With the name of a city, Latin leaves out the preposition. The case on its own says whether you are at the city, going to it, or coming from it.

LatinCaseEnglish
Romaelocativeat Rome, in Rome
Romamaccusativeto Rome
Romaablativefrom Rome
nuntius Romae est.
The messenger is in Rome.
Romae is locative: "at" or "in Rome".
the placethe length of time
amici Romam navigant.
The friends sail to Rome.
There is no ad: the accusative alone means "to".
  • The locative is a sixth case, used for where something happens with cities, towns and small islands.
  • For a 1st declension city like Roma, the locative ends in -ae, the same as the genitive.
  • Rome is the city on the word list. Other cities in passages are glossed, and they work in the same way.

Time how long

To say how long something goes on, Latin puts the length of time in the accusative, again with no preposition. English adds "for".

servi quinque horas laborant.
The slaves work for five hours.
horas is accusative plural: how long they work.
puer horam dormit.
The boy sleeps for an hour.
  • horam, "for an hour"; annum, "for a year".
  • quinque, five, and decem, ten, never change their endings: quinque horas, decem annos.
  • The accusative of time answers the question "for how long?" The object of the verb is a separate word.

Translating them

  • A city with no preposition: look at the ending. -ae is "at", -am is "to", -a is "from".
  • An accusative of hours or years: add "for".
viri Roma veniunt.
The men come from Rome.
dominus Romae decem annos laborat.
The master works in Rome for ten years.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • With a verb of being, staying or working, Romae is "at Rome" or "in Rome", even though it looks like the genitive.
  • Romam is "to Rome" even without ad.
  • Roma after a verb of coming or sailing is "from Rome".
  • horas and annos say how long: add "for", as in "for five hours".

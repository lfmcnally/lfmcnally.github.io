Prepositions with the ablative

a, ab, cum, de, e, ex and in: prepositions that put the noun after them in the ablative.

Prepositions that take the ablative

The prepositions in Chapter 1 took the accusative, and most of them meant movement towards something. These take the ablative. They mostly mean being somewhere, or moving away from it.

servus cum amico ambulat.
The slave walks with a friend.
cum takes the ablative: amico, not amicum.
the prepositionits noun, in the ablative
  • The noun after a, ab, cum, de, e, ex and in meaning "in" is in the ablative: -a or -o in the singular, -is in the plural.
  • a and e are used before a consonant, ab and ex before a vowel: a villa, ab insula; e villa, ex insula.
  • Translate the preposition and its noun together, as one phrase.

The prepositions

LatinEnglishExample
a, abfrom, away froma villa: away from the house
cumwith, together withcum servis: with the slaves
dedown from, aboutde muro: down from the wall
e, exout of, frome taberna: out of the shop
inin, onin horto: in the garden
There are two ways to say "with". cum means "together with", usually a person: cum amico. The ablative on its own means "with" a thing you use: gladio, "with a sword".

The two meanings of in

in takes either case, and the case changes the meaning. With the accusative it is movement, "into". With the ablative it is position, "in" or "on".

servi in horto laborant.
The slaves are working in the garden.
horto is ablative, so they are in the garden already.
servi in hortum ambulant.
The slaves walk into the garden.
hortum is accusative, so they are going into it.

Translating the phrase

  • Find the preposition, then the noun after it. Check the noun's case only when the preposition is in.
  • Keep the two together in English: "out of the shop", "with his friends".
  • Latin often leaves out "his" or "her". Add it when English sounds better with it.
nuntius e villa ambulat.
The messenger walks out of the house.
servus de muro clamat.
The slave shouts down from the wall.
dominus cum amicis ambulat.
The master walks with his friends.
amicis is ablative plural. "His" is added for the English.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • in with the ablative is "in" or "on". With the accusative it is "into". Look at the ending of the noun.
  • e, ex is "out of"; a, ab is "away from". Both can be "from".
  • cum is "with" a person. A thing used, like gladio, needs no preposition.
  • de is "down from" with a place, and "about" with a story or a letter.

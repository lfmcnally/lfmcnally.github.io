Lesson 2.6Cases and conjugations

What the principal parts are

The GCSE word list gives each verb in several forms at once. These are its principal parts. Together they tell you which conjugation the verb belongs to, and later in the course they give you its other tenses.

PartExampleWhat it is
1stmoneo"I warn": the present tense
2ndmonere"to warn": the infinitive
3rdmonui"I warned": the perfect tense, in Chapter 4
4thmonitus"warned": a participle, in Book 2
  • Some verbs have only three parts: venio, venire, veni.
  • For now you need the first two. They tell you the conjugation.
  • Nouns work in the same way: the word list gives the genitive, servus, servi, and that shows the declension.

Telling the conjugation

Conjugation1st part2nd partThe clue
1stportoportare-are
2ndmoneomonere-eo and -ere
3rdtrahotrahere-o and -ere
4thaudioaudire-io and -ire
The pair to watch is the 2nd and the 3rd: both infinitives end in -ere. The first part decides. video has an e before the -o, so it is 2nd; duco has not, so it is 3rd.

Why it matters

The conjugation tells you which vowel the verb uses, and so which ending to expect, or to write.

servi equos trahunt.
The slaves drag the horses.
traho, trahere: 3rd conjugation, so "they" is -unt.
viri dominum monent.
The men warn the master.
moneo, monere: 2nd conjugation, so "they" is -ent.
feminae in villa sedent.
The women sit in the house.
sedeo, sedere: 2nd conjugation, so "they" is -ent.

Watch out

Common mistakes
  • An infinitive in -ere could be 2nd or 3rd conjugation. Check the first part: -eo is 2nd.
  • The first part includes "I": moneo is "I warn".
  • The third part is a different tense. monui is "I warned": leave it until Chapter 4.
  • inquit, "he says", has no principal parts to learn. It only ever appears in that form, inside a quotation.

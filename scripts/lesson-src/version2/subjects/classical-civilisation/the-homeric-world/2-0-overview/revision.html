Life in the Mycenaean Age
What this topic covers, mapped to the specification
What this topic is about
  • Topic 1 looked at the citadels from outside; this topic goes inside, to how people actually lived
  • it covers the palace at the heart of the city, the hunting and warfare of the elite, chariots, clothing and trade, and the Linear B tablets
  • nearly all the evidence is material (frescoes, objects, tablets, sites), so a running theme is what these remains can and cannot tell us

Working with sources
What to know about every source, and the problems of each type
For every prescribed source, know
  • appearance, style and content: what it looks like, how it was made, what it shows, in detail
  • original location: where it was found, and what kind of place that was (a grave, a shrine, a palace)
  • original purpose, and how that purpose shaped it (a luxury dagger made for display has costly materials and a heroic scene)
  • context: what it tells us about Mycenaean society
  • interpretations: what it meant to its original audience, and what it means to modern historians

Knowing the key issues with each type of source is part of the specification. Use these points to judge usefulness.

The exam
The paper, the question types, and how marks are earned
The paper
  • The Homeric World is one paper of 1 hour 30 minutes, worth 90 marks: Culture (this half) and Literature (the Odyssey), 45 marks each
  • the Culture questions are built on sources: the prescribed ones, and also unseen ones, which can be visual, material or literary
  • questions range from short answers to extended responses
The question types and what to do
  • identify / name / give (1 to 2 marks): a precise fact, no explanation needed
  • describe (2 marks or so): two accurate details from the source or your knowledge
  • explain (2 to 3 marks): a point plus why (“because...”, “which shows...”)
  • 8-mark source question, often “How useful is this source for...?” or “How far does this source show...?”: use the source (quote or name its details) and your own knowledge; pair each fact with what it shows; end with a judgement
  • 15-mark essay: a statement to agree or disagree with, using a range of sources; argue both sides, make each point with evidence and analysis, and reach a clear conclusion
The two skills that earn the marks
  • AO1, knowledge and understanding: accurate, detailed facts about the sources, their context, and how different audiences have read them
  • AO2, analysis and evaluation: saying what the evidence shows, how reliable or useful it is, and building an argument
  • the simplest way to show both: a fact, then a sentence on what it shows (“the hunters carry huge body-shields, which shows hunting used the same equipment as war”)
Unseen sources
  • decide what kind of source it is (a fresco, a pot, a weapon, a passage of Homer) and when it is from
  • describe what you can see or read, using the right terms
  • link it to the prescribed sources and topics you know (“like the Warrior Vase, this shows...”)
  • judge it as you would any source: who made it, for whom, and what it cannot show
  • any appropriate source earns credit in “range of sources” questions, so learn the other evidence sections of each lesson too
The big idea
Palace, elite and trade
The big idea
  • the palace was the centre: it housed the ruler, stored the wealth, kept the records and hosted the feasts
  • the elite defined themselves through hunting, war and display, and trade brought in the metals and luxuries Greece lacked
  • because so much of the evidence is art and objects made for the elite, we are always judging how reliable a picture it gives
Everything in this topic radiates from the palace: home, treasury, office, workshop and shrine at once, with an elite around it who lived to hunt, fight and show their wealth.
